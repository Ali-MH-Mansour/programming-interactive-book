Before we start

This first chapter has no code in it. It is the map of the whole course — read it once now, and come back to the grading and lab-rules sections before your first graded test.

This is a first-year Programming (Программирование) course at Southern Federal University, 2026–2027, taught through C and C++. The lead lecturer is Ali Mahmoud Mansour (mansur@sfedu.ru). The course home is the ИКТИБ LMS, course “Программирование (2026-2027)”.

§1 · What you will get from this course

  • A foundation for learning any other programming language.
  • Correct use of the data types and algorithmic constructs found in most languages (taught through C/C++).
  • Code-reading and debugging skills; coding-style discipline; awareness of portability and cross-platform issues.
  • Multithreading for performance; creating and linking libraries.
  • C knowledge at the level of international certification exams (cppinstitute.org): CLE — 100% coverage; CLA — 80–90%.
  • Hands-on familiarity with: Code::Blocks, VSCodium, CMake, Doxygen, Codeforces, Diagrams.net, Far manager, Moodle.

§2 · Why C (and not Python/Java/C++ first)?

C is simple enough to master in one semester, teaches you how the machine actually works, is the base for both higher-level (C++, Java) and lower-level (assembler) study, and still runs the world:

Written in C: Linux, Git, CPython, PostgreSQL, SQLite, Redis, the Windows kernel, and the macOS kernel.

It gives the programmer great freedom — and punishes mistakes severely. That is the point.

§3 · How the course runs

Each topic is roughly a two-week cycle. The same five stages repeat for every topic — so once you know the rhythm, you know what to expect all semester.

§4 · Graded test (lab) rules

Read these carefully.

⚠️ Warning: A student who learns these rules after the first test may already have lost real points. Read them now.
  • Limited time (one class period), 3–6 problems of varying difficulty. Being late does not extend your time.
  • No phones. At all.
  • Screen recording for the entire session is mandatory (OBS Studio or similar). No recording = 0 points. The file is handed to the instructor on request. Test your setup at home before the first lab.
  • Allowed resources only: the course slide decks, cprogramming.com, an ASCII table, and the judge platform (Codeforces / Yandex.Contest).
  • Plagiarism and AI-use checks run after every test.
  • The judge allows unlimited submissions per problem and counts partial solutions; points transfer to the gradebook only above a threshold:
    • 60–74 judge points → 0.5 gradebook points
    • 75–99 judge points → 0.75 gradebook points
    • 100 judge points → 1.0 gradebook points
  • Your own laptop is allowed only if: screen faces the instructor, light theme, taskbar always visible, screen recorder installed.
  • Coding-style requirements are mandatory unless the problem statement says otherwise.

The exam

About 4 hours, written, no oral part:

  • 24-question test on all topics — 12 pts, ~40–60 min
  • 2–3 programming problems with automatic judging — 20 pts, ~90–120 min
  • One theory question answered in detail — 8 pts, ~30 min

Retakes during the semester: one extra attempt per missed or failed test. Two retake periods are planned — November (entrance + Tests 1–2) and January (Tests 3–5).

⚠️ Retaking is a real decision, not a free upgrade. If you already have below-threshold points and choose to retake, your old points are zeroed regardless of the retake's outcome.

§7 · Entrance testing — first weeks of term

  • Informatics entrance test: written, on paper — Wednesday, September 9. Check LMS for room assignments.
  • Programming entrance test: on PCs, during lab slots — September 7–12.
  • Watch the Codeforces mini-tutorial video first (link in the LMS). Register your Codeforces account this week, before the test.

§8 · Extra opportunities

  • Optional companion course “Programming practicum”: walkthroughs of homework and tests + online mock tests before each real one (pass/fail; Fair-Play rules, plagiarism/AI checks apply). Recommended for anyone who struggled with the entrance test.
  • Bonus points for competitive programming (RuCode, ICPC stages, SFedU freshman tournament “CodeCombat”), for completing the Moodle course “Основы разработки профессиональных приложений на C/C++”, or for helping prepare lab problem sets (advanced students, by competition — agreed in advance).
  • Consultations: at the instructor's posted office hours. Best format: e-mail your questions in advance, then come. Not for point-grubbing.

§9 · Track yourself

Every 2 weeks, compute your percentage of the maximum points available so far. If the number is lower than you expected: add study hours, come to consultations, read more, and solve — solve — solve.

Self-study norm: 4–6 hours per week.
